## Setting

`r` is odd; fix `c ∈ μ^d`, extended by `c_0`, and a set `ℛ` of representatives. Let

`I := (ψ_J : J a matching) ⊆ F[G]`.

**Identification.** `R_c = B({1, …, d})` (both are `F[t_1, …, t_d]/((t_i − c_i)^q)`); below, `π_c(I)` is regarded as an ideal of `B({1, …, d})`.

**Blocks of variables.** Put `W_1 := 𝒞_1 ∖ {0}` and, for `ζ ∈ ℛ`, `W_ζ := (𝒞_ζ ∪ 𝒞_{ζ^{−1}}) ∖ {0}` (sets of variable indices in `{1, …, d}`). By Lemma 6.3(i), `W_1` and the `W_ζ` (`ζ ∈ ℛ`) are pairwise disjoint with union `{1, …, d}`.

**Pair factors.** For a pair `{a, b}` of elements of `V` with `a < b`, put

`f_{a,b} := t_b − 1` if `a = 0`,   `f_{a,b} := (t_b − 1)·(t_a t_b − 1)^{q−1}` if `a > 0`.

It involves only the variables `t_a` (if `a > 0`) and `t_b`.

**Block generators.** For a perfect matching `P` of `𝒞_1` (a fixed-point-free involution), `g_P ∈ B(W_1)` is the product of `f_{a,b}` over the pairs `{a < b}` of `P`. For `ζ ∈ ℛ` and a bijection `σ : 𝒞_ζ → 𝒞_{ζ^{−1}}`, `g_σ ∈ B(W_ζ)` is the product of `f_{min(a,σ(a)), max(a,σ(a))}` over `a ∈ 𝒞_ζ`. (All the variables occurring lie in the block: a pair of `P` lies in `𝒞_1`, a pair `{a, σ(a)}` lies in `𝒞_ζ ∪ 𝒞_{ζ^{−1}}`, and `0` is never a variable.) Empty products are `1`. Put

`I_{c,1} := (g_P : P) ⊆ B(W_1)`,   `I_{c,ζ} := (g_σ : σ) ⊆ B(W_ζ)`.

## Proposition 6.5

**(i)** If no matching is compatible with `c`, then `π_c(I) = 0`.

**(ii)** If some matching is compatible with `c`, then `π_c(I)` is the ideal of `B({1, …, d})` generated by the products `ι_{W_1}(g_P)·Π_{ζ∈ℛ} ι_{W_ζ}(g_{σ_ζ})`, over all perfect matchings `P` of `𝒞_1` and all families of bijections `σ_ζ : 𝒞_ζ → 𝒞_{ζ^{−1}}` (here `ι_W := ι_{W,{1,…,d}}`).

**(iii)** In both cases, `dim_F π_c(I) = dim_F I_{c,1}·Π_{ζ∈ℛ} dim_F I_{c,ζ}` when some matching is compatible with `c`, and `dim_F π_c(I) = 0` otherwise.

## Proof

By Lemma 6.1(iii), `π_c(I)` is generated by the elements `π_c(ψ_J)`.

*A matching `J` with `π_c(ψ_J) ≠ 0` is compatible.* By Lemma 6.2(iii), `π_c(ψ_J) ≠ 0` forces `c_a c_{J(a)} = 1` for every pair with `0 < a < J(a)`, i.e. for every `a` with `a ≠ 0` and `J(a) ≠ 0`; since `c_0 c_1 ⋯ c_d = 1`, Lemma 6.3(v) says that `J` is compatible.

(i) If no matching is compatible, every generator `π_c(ψ_J)` is `0`.

(ii) For a compatible `J`, Lemma 6.2(iii) gives `π_c(ψ_J) = w_J·Π_{a < J(a)} f_{a,J(a)}` with `w_J` a unit (the product in (6.2) is exactly `Π_{a<J(a)} f_{a,J(a)}`). By Lemma 6.3(iii), `J` corresponds to `(P, (σ_ζ))` with `P = J|_{𝒞_1}` and `σ_ζ = J|_{𝒞_ζ}`; the pairs of `J` are the pairs of `P` together with the pairs `{a, σ_ζ(a)}`, `a ∈ 𝒞_ζ`, `ζ ∈ ℛ`, each exactly once (the classes `𝒞_1`, `𝒞_ζ`, `𝒞_{ζ^{−1}}` partition `V`). Hence `Π_{a<J(a)} f_{a,J(a)} = ι_{W_1}(g_P)·Π_ζ ι_{W_ζ}(g_{σ_ζ})`. Conversely every `(P, (σ_ζ))` comes from a compatible `J` (Lemma 6.3(iii)). So the non-zero generators of `π_c(I)` are, up to units, exactly the products of the statement, and the incompatible `J` give `0`. By Lemma 6.4(v), multiplying generators by units does not change the ideal.

(iii) The second case is (i). In the first case, apply Lemma 6.4(iv) to the partition `{1, …, d} = W_1 ⊔ ⊔_{ζ∈ℛ} W_ζ` with the generating sets `E_1 := {g_P}` and `E_ζ := {g_σ}`: the ideal of (ii) has dimension `dim I_{c,1}·Π_ζ dim I_{c,ζ}`. ∎

## Checks

- Exact linear algebra in `R_c` over `F_p` (`μ ⊆ F_p^×` the `r`-th roots of unity, `q = p`, `k = 1`), computing `π_c(I)` from the `ψ_J` themselves (with `φ` expanded) and the block ideals from the `g_P`, `g_σ`: every colouring for `(p, q, r) = (7, 7, 3)`, and random samples (6 compatible and 2 incompatible colourings) for `(13, 13, 3)` and `(11, 11, 5)`: 43 colourings, `dim π_c(I) = dim I_{c,1}·Π_ζ dim I_{c,ζ}` for every compatible one and `π_c(I) = 0` for every incompatible one; 0 failures.
