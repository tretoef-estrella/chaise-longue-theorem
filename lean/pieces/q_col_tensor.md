# Tensor products of ideals, and box rings over a partition of the variables (§6.3 of the paper, Lemma 6.4)

This file is self-contained. It prepares Proposition 6.5 of the paper (the tensor decomposition of `π_c(I)`), which will use part (iv) below with the blocks of a colouring.

## Setting

`F` is a field. All algebras are commutative `F`-algebras, all tensor products are over `F`, and `dim` is the `F`-dimension.

**Box rings.** Let `d ≥ 0`, `q ≥ 1`, and `c = (c_1, …, c_d) ∈ F^d`. For a finite set `W ⊆ {1, …, d}` put

`B(W) := F[t_i : i ∈ W] / ((t_i − c_i)^q : i ∈ W)`

(for `W = ∅`, `B(∅) = F`). For `W' ⊆ W`, let `ι_{W',W} : B(W') → B(W)` be the `F`-algebra map induced by `t_i ↦ t_i` (well defined: each relation `(t_i − c_i)^q` of `B(W')` is a relation of `B(W)`). The ring `R_c` of `q_col_splitting.md` is `B({1, …, d})`.

## Lemma 6.4

**(i) (tensor products of ideals)** Let `A`, `A'` be finite-dimensional algebras and `I = (E) ⊆ A`, `I' = (E') ⊆ A'` ideals generated by sets `E`, `E'`. Then the ideal of `A ⊗ A'` generated by `{e ⊗ e' : e ∈ E, e' ∈ E'}` is the image of `I ⊗ I' → A ⊗ A'`, this map is injective, and the ideal has dimension `dim I · dim I'`.

**(ii) (box rings split)** If `W = W_1 ⊔ W_2` (disjoint union), the `F`-algebra map `B(W_1) ⊗ B(W_2) → B(W)`, `x ⊗ y ↦ ι_{W_1,W}(x)·ι_{W_2,W}(y)`, is an isomorphism.

**(iii) (two blocks)** If `W = W_1 ⊔ W_2`, and `I_1 = (E_1) ⊆ B(W_1)`, `I_2 = (E_2) ⊆ B(W_2)` are ideals generated by sets `E_1`, `E_2`, then the ideal of `B(W)` generated by `{ι_{W_1,W}(e_1)·ι_{W_2,W}(e_2) : e_1 ∈ E_1, e_2 ∈ E_2}` has dimension `dim I_1 · dim I_2`.

**(iv) (finitely many blocks)** Let `W = W_1 ⊔ ⋯ ⊔ W_s` be a partition of `W` into pairwise disjoint sets (`s ≥ 0`; for `s = 0`, `W = ∅`), and for each `j` let `I_j = (E_j) ⊆ B(W_j)` be an ideal generated by a set `E_j`. Then the ideal of `B(W)` generated by

`{ Π_{j=1}^{s} ι_{W_j,W}(e_j) : e_j ∈ E_j for every j }`

has dimension `Π_{j=1}^{s} dim I_j` (for `s = 0`: the ideal generated by the empty product `1`, which is `B(∅) = F`, of dimension `1`).

**(v) (units)** If every generator of an ideal is multiplied by a unit of the ring, the ideal generated does not change.

## Proof

(i) `I ⊗ I'` maps into `A ⊗ A'`; the map is injective because over a field `I → A` and `I' → A'` are injective and tensoring with a vector space preserves injectivity (`F` is a field, so every module is flat). Its image `M` is an ideal: it is spanned by pure tensors `x ⊗ x'` (`x ∈ I`, `x' ∈ I'`), and `(r ⊗ r')(x ⊗ x') = rx ⊗ r'x' ∈ M`. It contains every `e ⊗ e'`. Conversely, for `x = Σ_α r_α e_α ∈ I` and `x' = Σ_β r'_β e'_β ∈ I'`, `x ⊗ x' = Σ_{α,β} (r_α ⊗ r'_β)(e_α ⊗ e'_β)` lies in the ideal generated by the `e ⊗ e'`; so `M` is that ideal. Its dimension is `dim(I ⊗ I') = dim I · dim I'`.

(ii) With `s_i := t_i − c_i`, `B(W)` has the `F`-basis `{Π_{i∈W} s_i^{b_i} : 0 ≤ b_i ≤ q − 1}`, and likewise `B(W_1)`, `B(W_2)`. The map sends the basis vector `(Π_{W_1} s_i^{b_i}) ⊗ (Π_{W_2} s_i^{b_i})` of `B(W_1) ⊗ B(W_2)` to the basis vector `Π_{W} s_i^{b_i}` of `B(W)`, and this is a bijection of bases (`W` is the disjoint union). It is an algebra map by construction.

(iii) Transport (i), with `A = B(W_1)`, `A' = B(W_2)`, along the isomorphism of (ii), which maps `e_1 ⊗ e_2` to `ι_{W_1,W}(e_1)·ι_{W_2,W}(e_2)` and maps ideals generated by a set onto ideals generated by its image.

(iv) Induction on `s`. For `s = 0` see the statement. For `s ≥ 1`, put `W' := W_1 ⊔ ⋯ ⊔ W_{s−1}` and let `I'` be the ideal of `B(W')` generated by `E' := {Π_{j<s} ι_{W_j,W'}(e_j)}`; by induction `dim I' = Π_{j<s} dim I_j`. Since `ι_{W',W} ∘ ι_{W_j,W'} = ι_{W_j,W}`, the generators of the statement are exactly the products `ι_{W',W}(e')·ι_{W_s,W}(e_s)` with `e' ∈ E'`, `e_s ∈ E_s`; apply (iii) to `W = W' ⊔ W_s`.

(v) If `u` is a unit, `(ug) = (g)`: `ug ∈ (g)` and `g = u^{−1}(ug) ∈ (ug)`. ∎

## Checks

- Exact linear algebra in `B(W)` over `F_p`, with random `c ∈ F_p^d` and random generating sets `E_j` (1–2 random polynomials in the variables of `W_j`), for `(p, q) ∈ {(3,3), (5,5), (3,9)}` and the partitions `{0}|{1}`, `{0}|{1,2}`, `{0}|{1}|{2}`, `{0,1}|{2}` (when `q^{|W|} ≤ 800`): the ideal generated by the products has dimension `Π_j dim I_j` in 72 of 72 trials.
- Negative control (the blocks must be disjoint): with generators of `I_1` in `t_0` and of `I_2` in `t_0, t_1` (overlapping variables), the product formula fails in 12 of 12 random trials.
