# Every tight pattern of a layer lies in the right slice (§7.5 of the paper, Proposition 7.5)

This file uses, unchanged:
- `q_bip_setting.md`: the ring `R_{α,β}` (box exponent `q`), the variables `x_i`, `z_l`, tight patterns of a pair of partitions and their products, the ideals `V_Λ`, `BPar_q(α, β)`, down-sets of `BPar_q(α, β)`, and the slices `W_j(V) ⊆ R_{α−1,β}` of Lemma 7.1 (peeling `w := x_1`) with its parts (i) (`W_j(V)` is an ideal) and (ii) (`W_j(V) ⊆ W_{j+1}(V)`);
- `q_bip_options.md`: the options `opt_p(μ)`, `F_Λ(μ)`, the layers `Λ_i`, and parts (i), (iv), (v) of Lemma 7.3;
- `q_P3_identities.md`: the Vandermonde `Δ(B)`, column lengths `λ'_c`, and parts (ii) and (iii) of its Lemma (identities with integer coefficients, valid in any commutative ring, in particular in `R_{α,β}`);
- `q_chain_lemma.md`: partitions, `μ − e_j`, `μ + e_j`, `μ ⊔ 1`.

## Setting

`F` is a field, `q ≥ 3` is odd, and the binomial coefficients `C(q−1, t)`, `0 ≤ t ≤ q − 1`, are non-zero in `F` (for instance `char F = 0`, or `char F = p` and `q = p^v`; the proof below only uses `C(q−1, r−1) ≠ 0` for some `1 ≤ r ≤ q`). `α ≥ 1`, `β ≥ 0`.

**The layer ring inside the big ring.** Peeling `w := x_1` identifies `R_{α,β} = R_{α−1,β}[w]/(w^q)`, where the variables of `R_{α−1,β}` are renamed: the variable `x_i` of `R_{α−1,β}` (`i ∈ [α − 1]`) is the variable `x_{i+1}` of `R_{α,β}`, and `z_l` is `z_l`. (This is exactly the identification used by the slices `W_j` of Lemma 7.1.) Every `f ∈ R_{α,β}` has a unique expansion `f = Σ_{j=0}^{q−1} f_j w^j` with `f_j ∈ R_{α−1,β}`; write `[w^j]f := f_j` and `deg_w f := max{j : f_j ≠ 0}`. Under this renaming, a tight pattern of `μ` on `([α − 1], [β])` becomes a tight pattern of `μ` on `({2, …, α}, [β])`, with the same product, and it does not involve the index `1`.

## Proposition 7.5

Let `Λ` be a down-set of `BPar_q(α, β)`. For every `i ∈ {0, …, q − 1}`,

`V_{Λ_i} ⊆ W_{q−1−i}(V_Λ)`,

where `V_{Λ_i} ⊆ R_{α−1,β}` is the ideal generated by the products of the tight patterns (on `([α − 1], [β])`) of the elements of the layer `Λ_i = {ν ∈ BPar_q(α − 1, β) : F_Λ(ν) > i}`.

## Proof

**Step 1 (reduction).** `W_{q−1−i}(V_Λ)` is an ideal of `R_{α−1,β}` (Lemma 7.1(i)), so it suffices to show that it contains the product `G` of every tight pattern `(P, (B^+_c), (B^−_c))` of every `μ = (μ_+, μ_−) ∈ Λ_i`, read inside `R_{α,β}` on the indices `({2, …, α}, [β])`. Put `ℓ_± := ℓ(μ_±)` and `Φ := F_Λ(μ) ≥ i + 1 ≥ 1`. Since `W_j(V_Λ) ⊆ W_{j+1}(V_Λ)` (Lemma 7.1(ii)) and `q − Φ ≤ q − 1 − i`, it suffices to show

`G ∈ W_{q−Φ}(V_Λ)`.   (∗)

To prove (∗) we exhibit `f ∈ V_Λ` with `deg_w f ≤ q − Φ` and `[w^{q−Φ}] f = κ·G` for a non-zero constant `κ ∈ F`; then `κG ∈ W_{q−Φ}(V_Λ)`, and `W_{q−Φ}(V_Λ)` is a subspace, so `G ∈ W_{q−Φ}(V_Λ)`. Every `f` below is a combination with coefficients in `F` of products of tight patterns (on `([α], [β])`) of the pair `λ := opt_Φ(μ)`, and `λ ∈ Λ` by Lemma 7.3(iv) (`Φ ≥ 1`), so `f ∈ V_Λ`. By Lemma 7.3(v) exactly one of the three cases (α), (β), (γ) holds.

**Case (α): `Φ > q − ℓ_+`.** Put `j_0 := q + 1 − Φ ∈ {1, …, ℓ_+}`; then `λ = (μ_+ + e_{j_0}, μ_−)` and `j_0` is the first row of its length (Lemma 7.3(v)). Let `c^* := μ_{+, j_0} + 1`. The column `c^*` of `μ_+` has height `j_0 − 1` (the rows `1, …, j_0 − 1` have length `≥ c^*`, the rows `≥ j_0` have length `≤ μ_{+,j_0} < c^*`); let `S := B^+_{c^*}` (and `S := ∅` if `j_0 = 1`, when `c^* = μ_{+,1} + 1` is a new column). Put the index `1` into `S`: the pairs `P`, the `x`-blocks `B^+_c` (`c ≠ c^*`) and `S ∪ {1}` in column `c^*`, and the `z`-blocks `B^−_c` form a tight pattern of `λ` on `([α], [β])` — column `c^*` of `λ_+` has height `j_0` and the other columns are unchanged, and the number of pairs `α − |λ_+| = (α − 1) − |μ_+|` is unchanged. Its product `f` involves `w = x_1` only through `Δ(x_{S ∪ {1}})`. Since `1 < s` for every `s ∈ S` and `|S| = j_0 − 1 ≤ q − 1` (the box exponent is `q`, so no power `w^e` with `e ≤ q − 1` is killed), part (iii) of `q_P3_identities.md` gives `deg_w f = |S| = j_0 − 1` and `[w^{j_0 − 1}] f = (−1)^{j_0 − 1} G`. And `j_0 − 1 = q − Φ`.

**Case (β): `ℓ_− < Φ ≤ q − ℓ_+`.** By Lemma 7.3(v), `Φ = q − ℓ_+` (so `ℓ_+ ≤ q − 1`) and `λ = (μ_+ ⊔ 1, μ_−)`. Put the index `1` into the block `B^+_1` (column `1` of `μ_+`, of height `ℓ_+`; if `μ_+ = ∅` this is a new column `1`, with block `{1}`). This is a tight pattern of `λ` on `([α], [β])`: column `1` of `λ_+` has height `ℓ_+ + 1`, the other columns and the number of pairs are unchanged. By part (iii) of `q_P3_identities.md`, its product `f` has `deg_w f = ℓ_+ ≤ q − 1` and `[w^{ℓ_+}] f = (−1)^{ℓ_+} G`. And `ℓ_+ = q − Φ`.

**Case (γ): `1 ≤ Φ ≤ ℓ_−`.** Put `r := Φ`; then `λ = (μ_+, μ_− − e_r)` and `r` is the last row of its length (Lemma 7.3(v)). So the column `c_0 := μ_{−, r}` of `μ_−` has height `r`; let `S := B^−_{c_0}`, `|S| = r`, and write `G = Δ(z_S)·G'`, where `G'` is the product of the other factors of `G` (it involves neither `w` nor any `z_b`, `b ∈ S`). For `b ∈ S`, the pairs `P ∪ {(1, b)}`, the `x`-blocks `B^+_c`, the `z`-blocks `B^−_c` (`c ≠ c_0`) and `S ∖ {b}` in column `c_0` form a tight pattern of `λ` on `([α], [β])`: the new pair has a new first index `1` and a second index `b` not used by `P`; column `c_0` of `λ_−` has height `r − 1` and the other columns are unchanged (if `r = 1` and `c_0 = μ_{−,1}`, the column `c_0` disappears from `λ_−` and the empty block `S ∖ {b}` is dropped, `Δ(∅) = 1`); and the number of pairs `α − |λ_+| = ((α − 1) − |μ_+|) + 1` has grown by one. Its product is `Δ(z_{S∖{b}})·(w − z_b)^{q−1}·G'`. With the signs `ε_b` of part (ii) of `q_P3_identities.md` (for the ordered set `S`), put

`f := Σ_{b ∈ S} ε_b Δ(z_{S∖{b}})·(w − z_b)^{q−1}·G' ∈ V_Λ`.

Only `(w − z_b)^{q−1}` involves `w`, with `w`-degree `q − 1 ≤ q − 1` (so nothing is killed by `w^q = 0`), and by the binomial theorem, for `0 ≤ t ≤ q − 1`,

`[w^{q−1−t}] (w − z_b)^{q−1} = C(q−1, t)·(−1)^t·z_b^t`.

Hence, by part (ii) of `q_P3_identities.md` (with `y_b := z_b`),

`[w^{q−1−t}] f = C(q−1, t)(−1)^t·(Σ_{b∈S} ε_b Δ(z_{S∖{b}}) z_b^t)·G'`,

which is `0` for `0 ≤ t ≤ r − 2` and `C(q−1, r−1)(−1)^{r−1}·Δ(z_S)·G' = C(q−1, r−1)(−1)^{r−1}·G` for `t = r − 1` (here `r − 1 ≤ ℓ_− − 1 ≤ q − 1`). So `deg_w f ≤ q − r`, and `[w^{q−r}] f = κG` with `κ := (−1)^{r−1}C(q−1, r−1) ≠ 0` in `F`. And `q − r = q − Φ`.

The three cases cover `1 ≤ Φ ≤ q` and are mutually exclusive (Lemma 7.3(v)), which proves (∗). ∎

## Checks

- Exact linear algebra over `F_p`, for every non-empty down-set `Λ` of `BPar_q(α, β)` and every `i` with `V_{Λ_i} ≠ 0`, testing each tight-pattern generator of `V_{Λ_i}` for membership in `W_{q−1−i}(V_Λ)`: cells `(q, α, β, p)` = `(3,1,1,3)`, `(3,2,1,3)`, `(3,1,2,3)`, `(3,2,2,3)`, `(3,3,1,3)`, `(3,1,3,3)`, `(3,2,3,3)`, `(3,3,2,3)`, `(3,1,4,3)`, `(5,1,1,5)`, `(5,2,1,5)`, `(5,1,2,5)`, `(5,2,2,5)`, `(7,1,1,7)`, `(7,2,1,7)`, and over `F_101`: `(3,2,2)`, `(5,2,1)`: 244 inclusions, 0 failures.
- Negative control (the slice is sharp): with `W_{q−2−i}` in place of `W_{q−1−i}`, at `(3,2,1)`, `(3,2,2)`, `(5,2,1)`, `(3,1,2)`: 26 failures out of 48.
- Negative control (the binomial hypothesis is needed): `q = 5` over `F_3`, where `C(4, 2) = 6 = 0`, at `(α, β) = (1, 3)`: 2 failures out of 26 (the construction of case (γ) with `r = 3` breaks). With the hypothesis restored (`q = 5` over `F_5`, or `(α, β) = (2, 2)` over `F_3`, where only `r ≤ 2` occurs): 0 failures.
