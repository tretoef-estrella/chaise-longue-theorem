# THE GLUE THEOREM
## The gap ideal is the Frobenius torsion of the gluing module: a dimension descent for the Sofa objective

**Operación Glotón / The Sofa Theorem · Constructor: Bisel · 7 July 2026 · English (Ley 30) · Princeton rigor / Quanta clarity (Ley 33).**
**Scope statement (honest, up front):** Theorems GF.1 and GF.2 below are proven in full for every tower level. They do **not** close Ley 1; they transfer the objective onto a strictly smaller-dimensional fixed object and are anchored at v=1 and v=2. Independent cold re-derivation (gate P0) is owed before any assembly into a closure.

### 0. Setting and notation

Work over `F₃`. Let `S = F₃[s₀,…,s₅]`, let `I_J` (J = 1,…,15) be the ideals of the 15 sheets `L_J ⊂ 𝔸⁶` (the matchings of K₆; each `L_J` a 3-plane, `I_J` generated by 3 linear forms), and let `E = (e₁,e₃,e₅) = ∩_J I_J` — a radical complete intersection (the Odd Symmetric Theorem, sealed pillar i). Set `R = S/E` (reduced, Cohen–Macaulay, dim 3).

Fix a **certified transverse frame** `ℓ₁,ℓ₂,ℓ₃ ∈ S₁` (Lemma F2, this session: F\* with its 15-determinant pencil certificate): the restrictions `ℓ|_{L_J}` form a coordinate system on every sheet. Let `B = F₃[ℓ₁,ℓ₂,ℓ₃] ⊆ R`; then `R` is free of rank 15 over `B` (Ley 39 platform), and for `q = 3^v`:
`C_q := B/(ℓ₁^q,ℓ₂^q,ℓ₃^q)`, `Λ := R/(ℓ^{[q]})R = R ⊗_B C_q` (dim 15q³, Gorenstein, socle degree 3q+3),
`A(q) := dim_{F₃} S/(E + m^{[q]})`, `gap(q) := length_Λ(s₁^q, s₂^q)Λ = 15q³ − A(q)` (Ley 39, sealed presentation).
Sealed anchors: `A(3)=141`, `A(9)=7761`; the objective (Ley 1) is `A(q) = P(q) = 15q³−45q²+55q−24` for all v, equivalently `gap(q) = 45q²−55q+24` for all v (an alias of the objective, never assumed).

### 1. The gluing module

Since `E` is radical, `R` embeds in the product of its component rings; since each `O(L_J)` is a polynomial ring (normal) this product is the **normalization**:
> `0 → R → R̃ := ⊕_{J=1}^{15} O(L_J) → Q → 0`  (exact),  `Q := R̃/R` — **the gluing module**.

Via the frame isomorphisms `π_J = (ℓ)|_{L_J} : L_J → 𝔸³`, each `O(L_J) ≅ B` as graded `B`-modules (degree-0 generator: the idempotent of the sheet); hence `R̃ ≅ B^{15}`, and `Q` is a finitely generated graded `B`-module, **generated in degree 0**, of Krull dimension ≤ 2 (it is torsion: `R` and `R̃` both have rank 15 over `B`), supported on the coincidence locus of the 15 sheets — a union of `F₃`-rational linear subspaces of `𝔸³` (45 planes from edge-sharing pairs and lines from the rest; census sealed this session). **Q is a fixed object: it does not depend on v.**

### 2. Theorem GF.1 (containment and identity)

> **For every `q = 3^v`:  (i) `(s₁^q, s₂^q)Λ ⊆ ker(Λ → ⊕_J O(L_J)/(ℓ|_J)^{[q]}) = Tor₁^B(Q, C_q)`;  (ii) `dim_{F₃} Tor₁^B(Q, C_q) = dim_{F₃} Q ⊗_B C_q = HK_Q(q)`, the Hilbert–Kunz function of the fixed module Q. Consequently `gap(q) ≤ HK_Q(q)` for all v.**

*Proof.* Tensor the defining sequence of §1 with `C_q` over `B`. Since `R` and `R̃` are free `B`-modules, the long exact sequence collapses to the four-term exact sequence
`0 → Tor₁^B(Q,C_q) → Λ → R̃⊗_B C_q → Q⊗_B C_q → 0`, with `R̃⊗C_q = ⊕_J O(L_J)/(ℓ|_J)^{[q]}`.
(i) Fix a sheet `J`. By transversality, `s_i|_{L_J} = λ_J^{(i)}(ℓ|_J)` for a linear form `λ_J^{(i)}` with `F₃` coefficients (Split Spectrum Lemma 1a). In characteristic 3, `(λ(ℓ))^q = λ(ℓ₁^q, ℓ₂^q, ℓ₃^q)` because the coefficients are fixed by Frobenius; hence `s_i^q|_{L_J} ∈ (ℓ|_J)^{[q]}`, i.e. `s_i^q` maps to `0` in every summand. Thus the ideal `(s₁^q,s₂^q)Λ` lies in the kernel, which the sequence identifies with `Tor₁^B(Q,C_q)`.
(ii) `dim Λ = 15q³ = dim R̃⊗C_q` (both free of rank 15 over `B`, tensored with the q³-dimensional `C_q`). The alternating sum of dimensions along the four-term exact sequence vanishes, giving `dim Tor₁ = dim Q⊗C_q`. Finally `gap(q) = dim (s₁^q,s₂^q)Λ ≤ dim ker = HK_Q(q)`. ∎

### 3. Theorem GF.2 (the equality criterion: the weak docking)

> **For every `q`, the following are equivalent:**
> **(a)** `gap(q) = HK_Q(q)`;
> **(b)** the induced map `R_q = S/(E+m^{[q]}) → ⊕_J S/(I_J + ℓ^{[q]})` is injective;
> **(c)** *(weak docking)* `∩_{J}(I_J + ℓ^{[q]}) ⊆ E + m^{[q]}`;
> **(d)** *(normalization form)* `m^{[q]}R̃ ∩ R = m^{[q]}R`.

*Proof.* (a)⟺(b): by GF.1(i), `(s₁^q,s₂^q)Λ ⊆ ker(Λ→R̃⊗C_q)`; equality of dimensions (a) holds iff the two coincide as subspaces, iff the quotient map `Λ/(s₁^q,s₂^q)Λ = R_q → Λ/ker = im ⊆ R̃⊗C_q` is injective, which is statement (b) (note `S/(I_J+ℓ^{[q]}) = O(L_J)/(ℓ|_J)^{[q]}`).
(b)⟺(c): the kernel of `S → ⊕_J S/(I_J+ℓ^{[q]})` is `∩_J(I_J+ℓ^{[q]})`; injectivity of the induced map from `S/(E+m^{[q]})` means exactly `∩_J(I_J+ℓ^{[q]}) ⊆ E+m^{[q]}`. (The reverse containment is automatic: `E ⊆ I_J` and, by the Frobenius-linearity used in GF.1(i) together with the Ley 39 bridge, `m^{[q]} ⊆ I_J + ℓ^{[q]}` for every `J`.)
(c)⟺(d): for `t ∈ S`, `t ∈ I_J + ℓ^{[q]} ⟺ t|_{L_J} ∈ (ℓ|_J)^{[q]} = m_{L_J}^{[q]}` (transversality plus Frobenius linearity make `(ℓ|_J)^{[q]}` the full Frobenius power of the sheet's maximal ideal). Hence membership in `∩_J(I_J+ℓ^{[q]})` says the image of `t` in `R̃` lies in `m^{[q]}R̃`, and membership in `E+m^{[q]}` says the image of `t` in `R` lies in `m^{[q]}R`. ∎

**Remark (why "weak").** The sealed alias lattice contains the *full docking* `∩_J(I_J+m^{[q]}) = E+m^{[q]}`, equivalent to the objective. Since `ℓ^{[q]} ⊆ m^{[q]}`, condition (c) is implied by the full docking: it is a strictly weaker-or-equal statement, and is **not** a member of the alias lattice.

### 4. The dimension descent and the anchors

> **Corollary.** If the weak docking holds for all v, then `A(q) = 15q³ − HK_Q(q)` for all v, and **Ley 1 becomes the statement `HK_Q(q) = 45q²−55q+24` for all v — a Hilbert–Kunz statement about ONE fixed module of dimension ≤ 2** (down from the dimension-3 ring), over the regular base `B`, with all support strata `F₃`-linear.

**Anchors (measured, byte-exact, two independent probes + the Architect's Mac):**
- v=1: the evaluation `Λ → R̃⊗C₃` has rank `1,5,15,29,40,36,15` by degree (the sealed A-footprint), total `141 = A(3)`; `dim Tor₁ = 264 = gap(3)`; `HK_Q(3) = 264 = 45·9−55·3+24`. **Equality (weak docking) holds at v=1.**
- v=2 (engine `hkq9_engine_v1`, validated at q=3, run on two machines with byte-identical output): total rank `7761 = A(9)`; `HK_Q(9) = 3174 = 10935−7761 = gap(9) = 45·81−55·9+24`. **Equality holds at v=2.**
Two anchors are not a theorem (Ley 35). The forced structure so far: `e_HK(Q) = 45` (Lemma 7: the q²-coefficient is the number of edge-sharing pairs, proven for all v).

### 5. What this theorem does and does not do

It **does**: transfer the whole objective, for the first time in the campaign, onto a fixed, v-independent, smaller-dimensional carrier (Q), with an exact equality criterion (the weak docking) strictly below the alias lattice, twice anchored. It **does not**: prove the weak docking for all v (the RUNG), nor the value of `HK_Q` (P3). §318 flag: `HK_Q` must never be closed by generic Hilbert–Kunz/FFRT theory (quasi-polynomial); only the linear-strata structure qualifies.

Adjacent graves distinguished: §0 full docking (implies, is not implied by, (c)); §318 (flagged above); §211/§231 (consistent — Q *is* the sealed global glue, now carrying the objective explicitly).
