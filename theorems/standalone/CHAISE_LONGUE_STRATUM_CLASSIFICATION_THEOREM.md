# THE STRATUM CLASSIFICATION THEOREM FOR THE MATCHING ARRANGEMENT
## Chaise Longue campaign — standalone deposit v1
**Author:** Lacassagne (constructor) · **Commissioned by:** Orfila (auditor, mission #24 asalto A3) · **Architect:** Rafa · 26 Jul 2026

---

## ABSTRACT

Let `K` be a field with `char K ≠ 2`, let `n = 2k+2`, and let `S = K[x_1,…,x_n]`. The matching
arrangement is generated by the hyperplanes `H_{ab} = {x_a + x_b = 0}`. Every flat (stratum) of the
arrangement is `F_G = {x : x_a + x_b = 0 for every edge ab ∈ G}` for a graph `G` on `[n]`.

> ### **THEOREM.** `dim F_G = #{bipartite connected components of G}`.
> ### Each bipartite component with parts `(A,B)` contributes the sub-line `x|_A = t`, `x|_B = −t`.
> ### Each non-bipartite component collapses to `0`.

The classification is **complete and exhaustive for every `k`**: there is no third kind of component.
Proof is two lines. Verified against rank computation on **1500 random graphs**, `n = 4,6,8,10,12`.

**⚠️ CRITICAL SCOPE (§4): this theorem does NOT imply condition (I) of `GAP 3`, and the attempt to
derive (I) from it is refuted with data in §3.** The theorem is a true and useful classification; the
inference "every stratum is free ⟹ Frobenius commutes with intersection" is **false**.

---

## 1 · THE THEOREM

**THEOREM 1.** With the notation above and `char K ≠ 2`, `dim F_G = b(G)`, the number of bipartite
connected components of `G` (isolated vertices count as bipartite components).

**Proof.** The conditions decouple over connected components, so fix a connected `G` on vertex set
`V`. Pick `v_0 ∈ V` and set `x_{v_0} = t`. Along any edge, `x_a + x_b = 0` forces `x_b = −x_a`, so
along any walk from `v_0` the value is `(−1)^{ℓ}t` where `ℓ` is the length of the walk. Consistency
is exactly the requirement that all walks between two vertices have lengths of equal parity, i.e.
that `G` contains no odd closed walk, i.e. that `G` is **bipartite**; in that case the solution space
is the line `{x|_A = t, x|_B = −t}`, of dimension 1. If `G` is not bipartite it contains an odd
closed walk, which forces `t = −t`, i.e. `2t = 0`, i.e. `t = 0` since `char K ≠ 2`; the component
contributes dimension 0. Summing over components gives `dim F_G = b(G)`. ∎

**Corollary 2 (pair types).** For two distinct perfect matchings `J, J'` of `[n]`, the union graph
is a disjoint union of shared edges and even cycles — always bipartite — so
`dim(L_J ∩ L_{J'}) = #components of J ∪ J'`. Sharing `s` edges and covering the rest by `c` even
cycles gives `dim = s + c`. **Every pairwise intersection of sheets is a coordinate sub-torus in the
sense of Theorem 1**; none collapses.

**Corollary 3 (`char = 2` is excluded, and essentially).** In characteristic 2 the bipartite/
non-bipartite distinction disappears (`t = −t` identically) and every component contributes
dimension 1. The campaign works over `F_3` and its tower, so the hypothesis holds throughout.

**1500 graphs, 1500 agreements, no exceptions.** Reproducer: `strata_law.py`.

## 3 · WHAT THE THEOREM DOES NOT GIVE — the refutation, with data

Mission #24 proposed to derive `GAP 3` condition (I),
`E + m^{[q]} = ⋂_J (I_J + m^{[q]})`, from this classification: each stratum is a sub-line, hence a
free/polynomial object, hence "clean", hence (I) by a Mayer–Vietoris / Čech argument over the
lattice of flats.

**This inference is false, and the counterexample is already measured.** In the letter `k = 2`
(`n = 6`, `q = 3`) the triple of sheets

>   `J₁ = {01,23,45}`, `J₂ = {02,14,35}`, `J₃ = {05,13,24}`

**breaks commutation** in degrees `3, 4, 5` (measured: `35 ≠ 37`, `106 ≠ 108`, `241 ≠ 243`;
reproducer `triple_split.py`, mission #22). Its strata, computed by Theorem 1:

| subset | components | bipartite | `dim` | verdict by the dichotomy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `J₁J₂` | 1 | 1 | 1 | clean (sub-line) |
| `J₁J₃` | 1 | 1 | 1 | clean (sub-line) |
| `J₂J₃` | 1 | 1 | 1 | clean (sub-line) |
| `J₁J₂J₃` | 1 | 1 | 1 | clean (sub-line) |

**Every stratum is a single bipartite component — the freest case the dichotomy admits — and the
commutation still fails.** Therefore:

> **Freeness of every stratum is NECESSARY but NOT SUFFICIENT for (I).**
> The obstruction is not carried by any individual stratum; it is carried by the way the strata are
> glued, exactly as recorded in the tomb `NERVE-EXACTNESS-IS-NOT-LOCAL` (mission #22).
> **That tomb DOES apply to the stratum decomposition**, contrary to the mission's premise.

**Second scope correction (transport error).** The proposal to read the tower relation as
`t^q ± 1` comes from the character-lattice setting of the Sweet Lie theorem, where the ambient
object is a **torus** (`t` invertible, characters in `Z/d`). Condition (I) lives in a **polynomial
ring** (the affine cone). On the sub-line `x|_A = t`, `x|_B = −t`, with `q` odd,

>   `m^{[q]}|_{F_G} = (t^q, (−t)^q) = (t^q, −t^q) = (t^q)`,

so the relation is `t^q`, **not** `t^q ± 1`. It is a nonzerodivisor either way, so the Tor argument
is not damaged — but the two settings are not the same category and the transport must be stated
with this correction.

## 4 · SCOPE — WHAT THIS THEOREM DOES *NOT* CLAIM

1. **It does not close `GAP 3`, nor condition (I), nor any part of either.** See §3.
2. It does not claim that non-bipartite strata are "clean" in any sense beyond having dimension 0;
   the campaign's notion of cleanliness for (I) is not established by dimension.
3. `char K ≠ 2` is used essentially (Corollary 3).
4. It says nothing about `GAP 2`, `GAP 4`, `GAP 5`, nor about the defect-module programme (§13 of
   the assembly draft, off the critical path).
5. The verification is `n ≤ 12` on random graphs; the theorem itself is proved for all `n`.
