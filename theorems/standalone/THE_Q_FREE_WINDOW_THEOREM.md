# THE q-FREE WINDOW THEOREM *(v3 — now covering `q \geq 2k+1`, band II included)*
### *For fixed `k`, the criterion for all `q > 2k+1` at once reduces to a fixed range of degrees*
### Bisel · Chaise Longue campaign · 2026-08-27 · **standalone, self-contained**

---

# 0 · WHY THIS DOCUMENT EXISTS

The conjecture `A_k(q) = P_k(q)` is indexed by two parameters, and the parameter `q = 3^v` runs over an infinite tower. Every reduction obtained so far had the same defect: it produced **a finite computation for each `q`**, and finitely many finite computations do not make a theorem.

This document removes `q` from the problem. It proves that, granted one generation statement, **the criterion for all `q > 2k+1` simultaneously is a condition in a fixed range of degrees `\leq 2k+1`, independent of `q`** — and that the target of that condition is the `q`-independent ideal `E`.

**Scope.** The range is `q\geq2k+1` — in this campaign, **bands II, III and IV**. An earlier draft (v1) claimed the same range but proved it only for `q>2k+1` **strictly**, excluding band II; v2 corrected the claim down to `q>2k+1`; **v3 proves the boundary case as well (Theorem 3.2, the Trio Law), restoring the full range.** *(The v1 error, its correction, and its repair are recorded rather than erased.)*

---

# 1 · SETUP

`K` a field of characteristic `3`, `q = 3^v`, `k\geq1`, `n = 2k+2`.
```
S = K[x_1,…,x_n],   E = (e_1, e_3, …, e_{2k+1}),   𝔪^{[q]} = (x_1^q, …, x_n^q),   R = S/E.
```
`e_j` = elementary symmetric polynomial, **odd ones only**. `E` is a homogeneous complete intersection of degrees `1,3,\dots,2k+1`, and it is **radical**; `R` is Cohen–Macaulay and Gorenstein of dimension `k+1`.

> **THE CRITERION.** For `b\in S^n` with `\sum_i b_ix_i^q \in E`, write `Λ(b) := \sum_i b_ix_i`. The conjecture in the cell `(k,q)` is equivalent to
> ### `\;Λ(b) \in E + \mathfrak m^{[q]}\;` for every such `b`.
*(This equivalence is established elsewhere in the campaign and is taken as the definition of the problem here.)*

---

# 2 · THE DESCENT LEMMA

> **LEMMA 2.1 (Descent).** `Λ` induces a well-defined `S`-linear map
> ### `\;λ \,:\, E \cap \mathfrak m^{[q]} \;\longrightarrow\; S/(E+\mathfrak m^{[q]})`, of degree `-(q-1)`,
> and **the criterion holds if and only if `λ = 0`.**

**Proof.** Let `φ : S^n\to S` send `\varepsilon_i\mapsto x_i^q`, and `ψ : S^n\to S` send `\varepsilon_i\mapsto x_i`; then `\operatorname{im}φ = \mathfrak m^{[q]}` and the set of admissible `b` is `φ^{-1}(E\cap\mathfrak m^{[q]})`. Since `(x_1^q,\dots,x_n^q)` is a **regular sequence** in `S`, `\ker φ` is generated by the Koszul relations `κ_{ij} = x_j^q\varepsilon_i - x_i^q\varepsilon_j`, and
```
ψ(κ_{ij}) = x_i x_j^q − x_j x_i^q  ∈  𝔪^{[q]},
```
every term carrying an `x^q`. Hence if `φ(b)=φ(b')` then `ψ(b)\equivψ(b') \bmod \mathfrak m^{[q]}`, so `λ(f) := ψ(b) \bmod (E+\mathfrak m^{[q]})` for any `b` with `φ(b)=f` is well defined. `S`-linearity is immediate from `φ(sb)=sφ(b)`, `ψ(sb)=sψ(b)`. The criterion is `ψ(φ^{-1}(E\cap\mathfrak m^{[q]}))\subseteq E+\mathfrak m^{[q]}`, i.e. `λ=0`. ∎

> **COROLLARY 2.2.** `λ(E\cdot\mathfrak m^{[q]}) = 0`, so the criterion is equivalent to the vanishing of `λ` on
> ### `\;T_q := (E\cap\mathfrak m^{[q]})\big/(E\cdot\mathfrak m^{[q]}) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Tor}_1^S(R,\,S/\mathfrak m^{[q]})`, **a module of finite length.**
*(For `f = e\,g\,x_i^q` with `e\in E`, take `b = eg\,\varepsilon_i`; then `ψ(b) = eg\,x_i \in E`. Extend by linearity. The identification is the standard `(I\cap J)/(IJ)\cong\operatorname{Tor}_1(S/I,S/J)`.)*

---

# 3 · ★ THE THEOREM

> **DEFINITION (Degree Law).** We say the **Degree Law** holds in the cell `(k,q)` if `E\cap\mathfrak m^{[q]}` is generated in degrees `\leq q+2k`.

> # **THEOREM 3.1 (`q`-free window).** Fix `k`. If the Degree Law holds in `(k,q)`, then for every `q > 2k+1` the criterion in that cell is equivalent to
> # `\;λ(g) \in E\;` for the finitely many generators `g`, a condition on elements of **total degree `\leq 2k+1`** — a range **independent of `q`** — and with target the **`q`-independent** ideal `E`.

**Proof.** `λ` is `S`-linear, so `λ=0` if and only if `λ` vanishes on a generating set of `E\cap\mathfrak m^{[q]}`. By hypothesis such a set sits in degrees `\leq q+2k`. Since `λ` has degree `-(q-1)`, the images `λ(g)` lie in degrees
```
≤ (q+2k) − (q−1) = 2k+1.
```
Finally, `\mathfrak m^{[q]}` is generated in degree `q`, so `(\mathfrak m^{[q]})_d = 0` for all `d < q`; if `q > 2k+1` then `2k+1 < q` and therefore
```
(E + 𝔪^{[q]})_d = E_d   for every d ≤ 2k+1.
```
Hence `λ(g)\in E+\mathfrak m^{[q]}` is the condition `λ(g)\in E`, imposed in the fixed degree range `[0,\,2k+1]`. ∎


> # **THEOREM 3.2 (the TRIO LAW — the boundary `q = 2k+1`).** Fix `k` and let `q = 2k+1`. If the Degree Law holds in `(k,q)`, the criterion in that cell is equivalent to the finitely many conditions, on generators `g` of `E\cap\mathfrak m^{[q]}`:
> # `\;d < q:\quad λ(g)\in E_d`, &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; `\;d = q:\quad λ(g)\in E_q + \langle x_1^q,\dots,x_n^q\rangle`,
> ### again in the fixed range `d\leq 2k+1`, independent of `q`, with the second case occurring **exactly once** — at the top degree `d = 2k+1 = q`, contributing `n = 2k+2` extra directions.

**Proof.** As in Theorem 3.1, the Degree Law puts the generators in degrees `\leq q+2k`, so their images `λ(g)` lie in degrees `\leq 2k+1`. The criterion is, and has always been, `λ(g)\in E+\mathfrak m^{[q]}`; Theorem 3.1 merely *simplified* it to `λ(g)\in E` using `(\mathfrak m^{[q]})_d = 0`, which requires `d < q`. Here `q = 2k+1`, so `(\mathfrak m^{[q]})_d = 0` for every `d\leq 2k`, and at the single remaining degree `d = 2k+1 = q` one has `(\mathfrak m^{[q]})_q = \langle x_1^q,\dots,x_n^q\rangle`. Substituting gives the two displayed conditions. No simplification is needed, and none is used. ∎

> ### **REMARK 3.4.** Theorems 3.1 and 3.2 together cover `q\geq2k+1` — the whole of bands II, III and IV. **The boundary case required no new mathematics: it required not simplifying the target.** The simplification `E+\mathfrak m^{[q]} \rightsquigarrow E` was a convenience of exposition in v1–v2, and mistaking a convenience for a hypothesis is what excluded band II for two versions of this page.

> ### **REMARK 3.2.** The bound is sharp in form: `2k+1 = n-1` is the degree of the top generator `e_{2k+1}` of `E`. The window is exactly as wide as the ideal that must receive the images.

> ### **REMARK 3.3 (what this does and does not do).** Theorem 3.1 removes `q` from the problem **conditionally on the Degree Law**. It does **not** prove the Degree Law, and it does **not** by itself close any cell: it converts *"infinitely many `q`, one finite check each"* into *"one check per `k`, in a fixed degree range"*. **That conversion is the content.**

# 4 · WHAT IS PROVED UNCONDITIONALLY ALONGSIDE

Two families are annihilated by `λ` by identity, for **all `k`, all `q`**, with no computation:
1. **Products.** `λ` kills `E\cdot\mathfrak m^{[q]}` — Corollary 2.2.
2. **Frobenius powers.** `e_r(x)^q = e_r(x_1^q,\dots,x_n^q)\in E\cap\mathfrak m^{[q]}`, and using `e_r(y)=\sum_i y_i\,e_{r-1}(y_{>i})` one gets `λ(e_1^q)=e_1\in E` and, for `j\geq1`, `λ(e_{2j+1}^q)=\sum_i x_i\,e_{2j}(x_{>i}^q)\in\mathfrak m^{[q]}`. **So `λ` kills the ideal `E^{[q]}\!\cdot\!S`.**
3. **Two power sums.** `q=3^v` is odd, so `q+2` and `q+2k` are odd; in `R` every odd power sum vanishes *(Newton's recursion in `R` descends by two, all odd `e_j` being zero, from `p_1=e_1=0`)*. Hence `p_{q+2}, p_{q+2k}\in E`; both lie in `\mathfrak m^{[q]}` since `x_i^{q+m}=x_i^q x_i^m`; and `λ(p_{q+m})=p_{m+1}`, giving `λ(p_{q+2})=p_3\in E` and `λ(p_{q+2k})=p_{2k+1}\in E`. **Both are killed by identity.**
