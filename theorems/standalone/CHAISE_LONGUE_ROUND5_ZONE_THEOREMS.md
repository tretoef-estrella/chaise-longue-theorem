## THEOREM A (pencil, two lines) — THE LOW HALF OF T IS DEAD, ∀k∀q
> **T_k(q)_e = 0 for every e < q, every k, every q.**
*Proof.* Let f ∈ T_e = ∩_p(L_p·R)_e with e < q. Fix any sheet V_J and pick a pair p ∈ J: then L_p ≡ 0 on the whole sheet, and lifting f = L_p·g_p + (E+m^{[q]}) — where the m^{[q]}-part is invisible below degree q — gives f|_{V_J} = 0. This holds for EVERY sheet (each contains some pair). By Radicality, f ∈ I(∪V_J) = E, so f = 0 in R. ∎

## THEOREM B (pencil, two lines) — CONTENT HOLDS BELOW DEGREE q, ∀k∀q (the high half of W=C)
> **∩_J (I_J + m^{[q]})_d = E_d for every d < q ⟺ W_c = C_c for every c > σ − q (σ = n(q−1)).**
*Proof.* Let z ∈ ∩_J(I_J+m^{[q]}) homogeneous of degree d < q. In each decomposition z = i_J + m_J, the m^{[q]}-part has no elements of degree < q, so z ∈ I_J EXACTLY, for every J; hence z ∈ ∩I_J = E (Radicality). The equivalence with W=C in high colon degrees is the Gorenstein duality of D (A'_d = dim W_{σ−d} via the Annihilator Theorem, unconditional). ∎
**Cross-check of pencils:** Theorem A is precisely the Matlis dual of Theorem B under the R×C pairing (Σ_p ker(L_p|C) ⊇ W fills C in high degrees ⟺ T dies in low degrees). Two independent two-line proofs of the same wall — double pencil.
