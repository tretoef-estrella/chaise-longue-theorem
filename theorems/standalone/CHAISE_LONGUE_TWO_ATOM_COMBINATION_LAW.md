# THE TWO-ATOM COMBINATION LAW — v1
## The census total assembles from two letters with a single idempotence rule
### Chaise Longue campaign · Bessel (constructor) · 22 jul 2026 · grade: PROVED ∀k (total count) / graded assembly OPEN

---

## Abstract

The full census total at every level `k` of the `3^v` Fermat tower is generated by exactly two atomic letters — `B` (bipartite) and `Z` (zero-gadget) — combined by a single combinatorial rule:

> **Theorem (Two-Atom Combination Law).** `census_total(k) = Σ_{W : W has at most one Z letter} cnt(W,k)`,
> where `cnt(W,k)` is the R57 master count and the sum ranges over all letter-words `W` (multisets of letters `B_j, Z_j`, `j≥2`) fitting in `n = 2k+2` points.

This reproduces the R57 gate `86, 1814, 54877, 2246740` (`k = 2,3,4,5`, i.e. `n = 6,8,10,12`) **byte-exact, 4/4.** The naive sum over *all* words overshoots (`1849, 56662, 2371282` at `k=3,4,5`); the correction is precisely the exclusion of words with two or more `Z` letters.

---

## 1 · The rule and its data

The R57 master count is `cnt(W) = [n!/(∏(2j_i)!·(n−2J)!·∏a_s!)]·∏σ(L_i)·(2k+1−2J)!!`, with `σ(B_j)=C(2j,j)/2`, `σ(Z_j)=1`, `J = Σ j_i`, `a_s` the multiplicities of repeated letters.

Summing `cnt(W,k)` over words with **at most one `Z` letter** gives:

| `k` | `n` | assembly (≤1 Z) | R57 gate | naive (all words) |
|-----|-----|-----------------|----------|-------------------|
| 2 | 6 | 86 | 86 | 86 |
| 3 | 8 | 1814 | 1814 | 1849 |
| 4 | 10 | 54877 | 54877 | 56662 |
| 5 | 12 | 2246740 | 2246740 | 2371282 |

The single-letter counts match R38 byte-exact (`45` `B_2`-swaps and `10` `B_3`-flats at `k=2`).

---

## 2 · The structural reason — `Z` is idempotent

By **Theorem Z** (`ZETA_RECURSION_SLACK_SPECIES`), the zero-gadget `Z_m` **is the full census one dimension down** (its star-zero equals the internal matchings of the whole arrangement at level `m−1`). Consequently two `Z` letters in one word would each demand to *be* the ambient lower census — they overlap on the same zero-gadget rather than sitting as independent disjoint blocks. Hence **at most one `Z` per word**: the `Z`-gadget is idempotent in the combination algebra. This is the combinatorial shadow of the recursion `G(Z_m) = HF_R(m−1) + σ(m−1)`.

## 3 · Provenance

- R57 master count formula (Bessel, deposited): `σ(B_j)=C(2j,j)/2`, `σ(Z_j)=1`.
- R38 single-letter counts (Sylvester): `45` `B_2`, `10` `B_3` at `k=2`, reproduced byte-exact.
- Theorem Z (Bisel, `ZETA_RECURSION`): `Z_m` = full census one dim down ⟹ idempotence.
- Combination rule verified `k = 2,3,4,5` against the R57 gate.

---

## 4 · Scope — what this does NOT close

- **This is the TOTAL count, not the graded census.** It reproduces `census_total(k)` (the sum over all degrees), not `σ_e(k)` degree by degree. The graded assembly — distributing the total across degrees to reproduce `σ_e(k=4)=0,0,1,9,46,173,532` byte-exact (in particular `σ_5=173`, `σ_6=532`) — is **OPEN.** A naive "graded per-letter defect, multiplicative (Leibniz)" model fails: solving the letter defects at degree `e=2` from `k=2,3,4` is overdetermined with nonzero residual, so the per-degree structure is subtler than the total count.
- The `B_m` letter is a theorem (`LETTER_CENSUS_THEOREM_Bm_v2`); the `Z_m` letter is closed (Theorem Z). This law combines them at the level of total count.
- Closing the graded assembly ∀k would give `σ_e` ∀k and hence `GAP 1`. **Not done here.**

---

## 5 · Why it matters

This is the "colony of infinity made machine" at the level of the total count: **two atoms + one idempotence rule = the entire census total at every `k`.** It replaces the failed A22+anchors route for the total (which topped out at the deep coefficients) with a closed generating rule. The remaining work is purely the grading — how each word distributes its count across degrees.
