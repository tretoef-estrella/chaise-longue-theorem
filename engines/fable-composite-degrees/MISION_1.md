## 1. The objective (why this matters)

Let `X ⊂ P^{2k+1}` be the complex Fermat variety `z_0^m + ⋯ + z_{2k+1}^m = 0` of dimension `n = 2k` and degree `m ≥ 3`. Let `L(X) ⊆ H_{2k}(X; Z)` be the subgroup generated by the classes of its standard `k`-dimensional linear subspaces.
- **Conjecture 1.2 of Degtyarev–Shimada** (arXiv:1405.4683, J. Math. Soc. Japan 68 (2016)): `H_{2k}(X; Z)/L(X)` is torsion free.
- Our paper (in this folder) PROVES it whenever `m = p^v` is a power of an odd prime, in every even dimension.
- **Your target:** every ODD degree `m`, prime power or not.

We have reduced the odd composite case, by hand, to one combinatorial-algebraic statement, the **bipartite count** (§3). The reduction is checked but not written out as a proof. The bipartite count is verified in 18 cells and proved in none.

**Winning levels:**
- **MINIMUM:** Part A written as a complete proof (the colour reduction), with every identification verified. This alone upgrades a hand check to a theorem.
- **GOOD:** Part A, plus the bipartite count PROVED for one infinite family: all `q` with `a ≤ 2`, or all `a` with `q = 3`.
- **EXCELLENT:** the bipartite count (balanced AND phantom) PROVED for every `a ≥ 0` and every odd prime power `q`. Then **Conjecture 1.2 holds for every odd `m`**.

## 2. The algebraic setting (from the paper, §2; our notation)

- `n = 2k`, coordinates `0, 1, …, n+1`, and `N := n+2 = 2k+2` of them.
- `𝒥` is the set of perfect matchings `J` of `{0, 1, …, n+1}`. Write the pairs as `{0, k_0}, {j_1, k_1}, …, {j_k, k_k}` (the pair containing `0` first).
- `R := Z[t_1, …, t_{n+1}]/(t_i^m − 1)`, the group ring of `G = (Z/m)^{n+1}`. **The variable `t_0` is NOT a variable.**
- `φ(u) := 1 + u + ⋯ + u^{m−1}`.
- `ψ_J := (t_{k_0} − 1) · Π_{i=1}^{k} (t_{k_i} − 1) φ(t_{j_i} t_{k_i})`.
- `Γ ⊆ μ_m^{n+1}` is the set of `(a_1, …, a_{n+1})` such that, with `a_0 := (a_1⋯a_{n+1})^{−1}`, all `a_i ≠ 1` (`i = 0, …, n+1`) and the multiset `{a_0, …, a_{n+1}}` splits into inverse pairs `{c, c^{−1}}`. (Paper, Lemma 2.2: this is `Γ_𝒥`.)

**Criterion (paper, Proposition 2.1, from [DS, Theorem 1.1] and Claim 4.3).**
- For every prime `p`: `dim_{F_p}(ψ̄_J : J ∈ 𝒥)F_p[G] ≤ |Γ|`.
- `H_{2k}(X; Z)/L(X)` is torsion free **iff equality holds for every prime `p` dividing `m`**.

Here `F_p[G] = F_p[t_1, …, t_{n+1}]/(t_i^m − 1)`, and the dimension is that of the ideal. It may be computed over `F̄_p`, since dimensions do not change under field extension.

So the target for each prime `p | m` is the inequality **`dim_{F̄_p}(ψ̄_J : J)F̄_p[G] ≥ |Γ|`**.

## 3. The colour reduction (PART A: write it as a complete proof)

Fix an odd `m` and a prime `p | m`. Write `m = q·r` with `q = p^v`, `p ∤ r`. Then `r` is odd.

**(A1) Splitting.** Over `F̄_p`, `t^m − 1 = (t^r − 1)^q = Π_{ζ ∈ μ_r}(t − ζ)^q`. By the Chinese remainder theorem,
`F̄_p[G] = Π_{c ∈ μ_r^{n+1}} R_c`,   `R_c := ⊗_{i=1}^{n+1} F̄_p[t_i]/((t_i − c_i)^q)`,
a product of local rings indexed by the **colourings** `c = (c_1, …, c_{n+1})`. Put `c_0 := (c_1⋯c_{n+1})^{−1}`.

**(A2) Which generators survive in `R_c`.** Verify each of these:
- `t_k − 1` is a unit in `R_c` if `c_k ≠ 1` (it is `(t_k − c_k) + (c_k − 1)`, a nilpotent plus a nonzero scalar).
- In characteristic `p`, `u^m − 1 = Π_{ξ∈μ_r}(u − ξ)^q`, so `φ(u) = (u − 1)^{q−1} Π_{ξ ≠ 1}(u − ξ)^q`.
- For `u = t_jt_k` in `R_c`: `(u − c_jc_k)^q = t_j^q t_k^q − (c_jc_k)^q = 0`. Hence the image of `φ(t_jt_k)` is `0` if `c_jc_k ≠ 1`, and `(unit)·(t_jt_k − 1)^{q−1}` if `c_jc_k = 1`.
- Hence the image of `ψ_J` in `R_c` is nonzero only if `J` is **compatible** with `c`: `c_{j_i}c_{k_i} = 1` for `i = 1, …, k`. Then also `c_0c_{k_0} = 1`, because the product of all the colours is 1.

**(A3) Blocks.** Partition `{0, …, n+1}` by colour:
- the **colour-1 class** `E := {i : c_i = 1}` (it contains `0` iff `c_0 = 1`);
- for each inverse pair `{ζ, ζ^{−1}}`, `ζ ≠ 1`, the classes `A_ζ := {i : c_i = ζ}` and `B_ζ := {i : c_i = ζ^{−1}}`. (Since `r` is odd, `ζ ≠ ζ^{−1}` for `ζ ≠ 1`.)

A compatible `J` is the same as an independent choice of:
- a perfect matching of `E`, and
- a bijection `A_ζ → B_ζ` for each pair `{ζ, ζ^{−1}}`.

Hence the ideal generated in `R_c` is the **tensor product of block ideals**, and its dimension is the product of their dimensions. Prove this: the ideal generated by the products `g ⊗ g'` in `R ⊗ R'` is `I ⊗ I'`.

**(A4) The colour-1 block.** With `y := t − t^{−1}` (paper, Lemmas 2.3–2.4) the block ring is `F̄_p[y_i : i ∈ E, i ≠ 0]/(y_i^q)`. The block generators are:
- **(if `0 ∈ E`)** exactly the generators of the paper's (S) at a smaller `k'` (`|E| = 2k'+2`), up to units. Its dimension is `≥ Q_{k'}(q)` = the block count, by the paper's Theorem B (Corollary 5.4). **Verify.**
- **(if `0 ∉ E`, `|E| = 2k'`)** `Y_E · D_P` for the perfect matchings `P` of `E`, with `Y_E = Π_{i∈E} y_i`. As in the paper's Proposition 2.5, their ideal has the dimension of `(D_P : P)C_{2k'}`, which is the paper's `V_Λ` for `Λ = {∅} ⊆ Par_{2k'}` (§5.2: a tight pattern of `∅` is a perfect matching). So its dimension is `≥ |Z_{{∅}}|` by Theorem 5.3. **Verify both identifications**, including that `{∅}` is a `≼`-down-set.
- The **block count** is the number of tuples in `(μ_q ∖ {1})^{E}` whose multiset is closed under inversion (with `a_0` included when `0 ∈ E`).

**(A5) A pair block `{ζ, ζ^{−1}}`.** Change variables:
- `x_i := ζ^{−1}t_i − 1` for `i ∈ A_ζ`;
- `z_j := (ζ t_j)^{−1} − 1` for `j ∈ B_ζ`.

Then `x_i^q = z_j^q = 0` and, for `i ∈ A_ζ`, `j ∈ B_ζ`: `t_it_j − 1 = (unit)·(x_i − z_j)`. **Verify.** So the block ideal is:
- **balanced** (`0 ∉ A_ζ ∪ B_ζ`, `|A_ζ| = |B_ζ| = a`):
  `I_a := (Π_{i=1}^{a} (x_i − z_{σ(i)})^{q−1} : σ ∈ S_a)` in `F̄_p[x_1..x_a, z_1..z_a]/(x_i^q, z_i^q)`;
- **phantom** (`0` in one class, say `B_ζ`, so `|A_ζ| = a+1` real variables and `|B_ζ ∖ {0}| = a`; the pair `{0, k_0}` contributes the unit `t_{k_0} − 1`):
  `I'_a := (Π_{i ≠ i_0} (x_i − z_{σ(i)})^{q−1} : i_0 ∈ [a+1], σ : [a+1]∖{i_0} → [a] a bijection)` in `F̄_p[x_1..x_{a+1}, z_1..z_a]/(box q)`.
  Check the symmetric case `0 ∈ A_ζ` as well.

**(A6) The counts factor the same way.** By the CRT, `μ_m = μ_q × μ_r`. A point `(a_i)` of `Γ` has a colouring (its `μ_r`-part) and `μ_q`-parts. Show that `Γ` is the disjoint union over compatible colourings, and that the number of points with colouring `c` is the product of:
- the colour-1 block count of (A4), and
- for each pair block, the **bipartite count**:
  - balanced: `N(a, q) := #{(ξ, η) ∈ μ_q^a × μ_q^a : multiset(ξ) = multiset(η^{−1})} = Σ_{c_1+⋯+c_q = a} (a!/Π c_s!)^2`;
  - phantom: `N'(a, q) := #{(ξ, η) ∈ μ_q^{a+1} × μ_q^a : multiset(η^{−1}) ⊆ multiset(ξ)}`.
  
  (Colours `ζ ≠ 1` make `a_i ≠ 1` automatic.) Also check that the phantom's `a_0` is consistent with the global product.

**(A7) Conclusion to prove.** If `dim I_a ≥ N(a, q)` and `dim I'_a ≥ N'(a, q)` for all `a` (for this `q`), then `dim(ψ̄_J : J) ≥ Σ_c Π(block counts) = |Γ|`, so Conjecture 1.2 holds at `m`. Only the inequality `≥` per block is needed, since the global `≤` is known.

**Gate for Part A (verify it yourself, from these definitions, before trusting it):**
- For `k = 1`, the literal dimension of `(ψ̄_J : J)` equals `|Γ| = 3(m−1)(m−2)`: `m = 15 → 546` (over `F_3`, `F_5` and `F_31`) and `m = 21 → 1140` (over `F_3`, `F_7`, `F_43`).
- The summed block counts must reproduce `|Γ|` exactly: for example at `(m, n) = (15, 2), (15, 4), (21, 2), (45, 2)`, `|Γ| = 546, 32900, 1140, 5676`. The last three come from our independent count of pair-type characters.

## 4. The bipartite count (PART B: the prize)

**Conjecture (bipartite count).** For every odd prime `p`, every `q = p^v` and every `a ≥ 0`, over `F_p`:
- (balanced) `dim_{F_p} I_a = N(a, q)`;
- (phantom) `dim_{F_p} I'_a = N'(a, q)`.

**Only `≥` is needed** (A7).

**Measured (two independent programs over `F_p`, `p` = the characteristic):**

| balanced `(a,q)` | `N` | phantom `(a,q)` | `N'` |
|---|---|---|---|
| (1,3) | 3 | (1,3) | 15 |
| (2,3) | 15 | (2,3) | 93 |
| (3,3) | 93 | (3,3) | 639 |
| (4,3) | 639 | (1,5) | 45 |
| (2,5) | 45 | (2,5) | 545 |
| (3,5) | 545 | (3,5) | 7885 |
| (2,7) | 91 | (1,9) | 153 |
| (2,9) | 153 | (2,9) | 3681 |
| (3,9) | 3681 | (2,7) | 1645 |

**Observation (measured, 9/9):** `N'(a, q) = N(a+1, q)`, and `dim I'_a = dim I_{a+1}`.
- The count identity has a one-line bijection: append the leftover element of `ξ` as `η_{a+1}^{−1}`. Prove it.
- Then look for a structural reason on the ideal side. If `I'_a` and `I_{a+1}` are related by an explicit map that preserves dimension, the phantom case costs nothing.

**What we know about routes (use it; do not repeat dead ends):**
1. **The paper's method.** Theorem 5.3 of the paper proves the analogous inequality for the colour-1 blocks by:
   - peeling one variable (Lemma 5.1);
   - enlarging the family to down-sets of partitions (residues under an involution);
   - proving three facts (P1, P2, P3) with no slack.
   
   **A bipartite analogue is the most natural route.**
   - The point side: pairs `(ξ, η)` with a «residue» `multiset(ξ) − multiset(η^{−1})` and its opposite.
   - The ideal side: products `Π (x_i − z_{σ(i)})^{q−1}` over a PARTIAL bijection, times something (Vandermonde-type?) in the unmatched variables.
   
   This is a suggestion, NOT a known result: the right family is for you to find, and to test on the table above.
2. **The evaluation route gives the wrong inequality.** Over `F_q`, `(x − z)^{q−1}` is the top form of `1 − [x = z]`, so the ideal of functions generated by the `f_σ` has dimension `#{points where some f_σ ≠ 0}`: 6 at `(a,q) = (1,3)`, against `N = 3`. Top forms of an ideal only bound `gr` from BELOW by containment, which gives `≤` for our ideal, the direction we do not need.
3. **Subfamilies of matchings fail in general** (paper, §7). The bipartite ideal uses ALL bijections `A → B`, and any proof must use that full symmetry.
4. `a = 1` is trivial: `((x − z)^{q−1})` has dimension `q = N(1, q)`, because `(x−z)^q = 0`. Start from it and try `a = 2` by pencil, for all `q`.
