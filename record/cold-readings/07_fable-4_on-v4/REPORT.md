# 6. THE WEIGHT OF THE THEOREM AND WHERE IT DESERVES TO BE PUBLISHED

*Journal facts below are from memory and may be out of date; where I am unsure of a scope I say so.*

**What is proved, in one sentence.** A conjecture stated in print in 2016 by two established specialists (Degtyarev, Shimada, J. Math. Soc. Japan), restated as open in 2015 by Degtyarev, and previously known only for surfaces (Degtyarev, J. Number Theory 2015) and for a handful of computed cells, is proved for all Fermat varieties of odd prime-power degree in every even dimension; with the classical rational statements of Shioda and Ran, this yields the integral Hodge conjecture for the Fermat varieties of odd prime degree (item 2 of section 5).

**How significant.** Honestly: significant inside its niche, invisible outside it. The topology and arithmetic of Fermat varieties is a small, old and respectable subject (Shioda, Katsura, Aoki, Ran, Schütt–Shioda–van Luijk, Degtyarev, Shimada). Within it, the primitivity of the lattice of linear cycles is *the* natural question after the rational Hodge conjecture, and the paper settles it for infinitely many degrees in every dimension. It does not settle the conjecture: composite `m` and `m = 2^v` remain open, and §9 explains why the method as it stands does not reach them (subfamilies), so the result is a large partial answer, not a closure. The integral Hodge corollary is the part that would be quoted by people outside the niche.

**Theorem, or method?** Both, and the method is the more lasting part. The theorem is a clean statement about a classical object; the method — the family `(V_Λ, Z_Λ)` indexed by down-sets under weak dominance, the fibre–slice matching, and the fact that the point sets `Z_Λ` have an explicit Tanisaki-type generating set for their vanishing ideal (Theorem 5.9, unconditional after section 5, item 1) — is a self-contained piece of combinatorial commutative algebra in the Garsia–Procesi tradition, and it is what a second paper could build on (coloured versions for composite `m`, §9). The cubic-case ballot basis (Theorem 4.1) is a pleasant bonus. Theorem A is a separate, smaller result.

**Weaknesses a referee will weigh.** (1) The proof is long, elementary and case-driven (Proposition 5.6 has seven cases; Theorem A.12 four); there is no conceptual reason yet why `V_Λ` should be the right ideal beyond the fact that the three lifting constructions land exactly in the slices. (2) The conjecture is only partly resolved. (3) The provenance: the paper states that the mathematics was developed with AI systems and that no human expert has refereed it. A referee will read more slowly and will ask for the exposition to be conventional. (4) The manuscript as it stands is long (≈100 KB), with a verification record and a history section that belong in a repository.

The integral Hodge corollary for an infinite family of Fermat varieties in all dimensions, plus a new Garsia–Procesi-type theorem, is the kind of pairing these journals publish; the case analysis and the partial resolution are what would sink it there.
