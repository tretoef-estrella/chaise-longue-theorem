# 2. Theorem 5.9: this is the actual crack

Theorem 5.9 says that when \(\operatorname{char}F\nmid q-1\),

$$
\dim_F V_\Lambda=|Z_\Lambda|.
$$

The proof has three pieces: extension of scalars, support, and degeneration.

The first two survive scrutiny.

## 2.1 Evaluation on the roots of unity

The paper takes \(T=\mu_{q-1}\).

This is legitimate after extending the field because the generators have integer coefficients, so the dimension of their span is unchanged by scalar extension.

Since \(\operatorname{char}F\nmid q-1\), \(y^{q-1}-1\) is separable, hence there are exactly \(q-1\) distinct roots. Since \(q-1\) is even and the characteristic is not \(2\), \(-1\neq1\).

All good.

The identity

$$
(a+b)D(a,b)=b^{q-1}-a^{q-1}
$$

then implies that on \(T\),

$$
D(a,b)=0
$$

unless \(b=-a\), while

$$
D(a,-a)=-(q-1)a^{q-2}\neq0.
$$

Correct.

The Vandermonde assertion is also exact: \(\Delta(B)\neq0\) precisely when the values on \(B\) are pairwise distinct.

So the characterization of the support of a tight-pattern generator is correct.

## 2.2 Support argument

The paper takes a point \(M\) on which a tight-pattern product attached to \(\lambda\) is nonzero and compares the multiplicity partition \(\nu'\) of the unpaired values to \(\lambda\).

The inequality

$$
S_t(\nu')\le \sum_c\min(t,\lambda'_c)=S_t(\lambda)
$$

is right: among the \(t\) most frequent values, column \(c\) can contribute at most \(\min(t,\lambda'_c)\) entries.

Then passing to the residue removes equal numbers of \(u\) and \(-u\), and replaces the pair of multiplicities \(a,\bar a\) by \(|a-\bar a|\). Since distinct residual values lie in distinct negation classes, these residual multiplicities can indeed be matched to distinct parts of \(\nu'\).

Thus the conclusion

$$
\lambda(M)\preceq\lambda
$$

is valid.

The paper then says the support union is actually equal to \(Z_\Lambda\). That also works: for a point whose residue shape lies in \(\Lambda\), use its maximal collection of negation pairs for \(P\), then distribute the residual entries one per row in the columns dictated by the Ferrers diagram of \(\lambda(M)\).

So there is **no failure in the support argument**.

At most, I would call the sentence "the parts ... are bounded, one by one ... hence \(\lambda(M)\preceq\nu'\)" a **PRESENTATION** issue: it compresses one majorization implication into a sentence that a hostile referee will force you to unpack.

## 2.3 The genuine error: the degeneration sentence

Here is the problematic sentence on PDF p. 14:

> "the initial ideal of \(I\) contains \((g_1,\ldots,g_s)+(y_1^{q-1},\ldots,y_m^{q-1})\)"

This is **literally false**.

The reason is basic Gröbner theory: \(\operatorname{in}(I)\) contains the **leading monomials** of elements of \(I\), not the elements \(g_i\) themselves.

The fact that \(g_i\) is homogeneous does **not** imply

$$
g_i\in\operatorname{in}(I).
$$

It only says that \(g_i\) is its own top-degree form.

### Explicit counterexample inside the paper's setting

Take the root case

$$
q=3,\qquad m=3,\qquad \Lambda=\{(1)\},\qquad F=\mathbb Q.
$$

Then the generators \(g_i\) are, up to signs,

$$
y_2-y_1,\quad y_3-y_1,\quad y_3-y_2.
$$

Set

$$
I=(y_2-y_1,y_3-y_1,y_3-y_2,\,
y_1^2-1,y_2^2-1,y_3^2-1).
$$

With lexicographic order \(y_1>y_2>y_3\), a Gröbner basis gives

$$
\operatorname{in}(I)=(y_1,y_2,y_3^2).
$$

But \(y_2-y_1\notin(y_1,y_2,y_3^2)\).

So the containment asserted in the proof is not merely insufficiently justified; it is false.

**Classification: GAP.**

I do **not** classify this as FATAL because the intended inequality is salvageable.

### The exact missing step

Put

$$
S=F[y_1,\ldots,y_m],\qquad G=(g_1,\ldots,g_s),
\qquad A=S/G.
$$

Because the \(g_i\) are homogeneous, \(A\) is graded.

Now let \(\bar f_i\) be the image of \(y_i^{q-1}-1\) in \(A\), and let \(J=(\bar f_1,\ldots,\bar f_m)\subset A\).

Use the degree filtration on \(A/J\). In the associated graded ring,

$$
\operatorname{gr}(J)
$$

contains the initial homogeneous parts \(y_i^{q-1}\). Therefore

$$
\operatorname{gr}(J)\supseteq
(y_1^{q-1},\ldots,y_m^{q-1})A.
$$

Consequently there is a surjection

$$
A/(y_1^{q-1},\ldots,y_m^{q-1})A
\twoheadrightarrow
\operatorname{gr}(A/J),
$$

and hence, because these are finite-dimensional,

$$
\dim_F A/(y_i^{q-1})
\ge
\dim_F A/J.
$$

But

$$
A/(y_i^{q-1})
=
C_m/V_\Lambda,
$$

while CRT gives

$$
\dim_F A/J=(q-1)^m-|\Sigma|.
$$

Since the support union \(\Sigma\) is \(Z_\Lambda\),

$$
\dim_F C_m/V_\Lambda
\ge (q-1)^m-|Z_\Lambda|,
$$

equivalently

$$
\dim_FV_\Lambda\le |Z_\Lambda|.
$$

Together with Theorem 5.3, equality follows.

That is the repair I would recommend. It is cleaner than trying to patch the Gröbner statement.

So my verdict on §5.9 is:

**The theorem itself looks right; the written proof has a genuine GAP at one sentence, and that sentence should be replaced, not merely footnoted.**

# 4. Theorem 5.3 and the induction

The quantifiers actually written are strong:

> For every field \(F\), every odd \(q\ge3\), every \(m\ge0\), and every \(\preceq\)-down-set \(\Lambda\subseteq\mathrm{Par}^{(h)}_m\),

$$
\dim_F V_\Lambda\ge |Z_\Lambda|,
\qquad h=\frac{q-1}{2}.
$$

This is indeed the theorem needed at the root \(\Lambda=\{(1)\}\).

The induction is on \(m\), **with \(F,q,T\) fixed**.

That matters, and the paper handles it correctly.

The induction step is:

$$
\dim V_\Lambda
=
\sum_{i=0}^{q-2}\dim W_i(V_\Lambda)
$$

then, after reversing the indexing,

$$
\ge
\sum_{i=0}^{q-2}\dim V_{\Lambda_i}
\ge
\sum_{i=0}^{q-2}|Z_{\Lambda_i}|
=
|Z_\Lambda|.
$$

The induction hypothesis can be applied because Proposition 5.6 establishes that every \(\Lambda_i\) is a down-set in \(\mathrm{Par}_{m-1}^{(h)}\).

I checked the dependence of the construction on the parameters: there is no finite-field hypothesis, no prime-power hypothesis, and no characteristic restriction. The role of \(q\) is combinatorial: \(T\) has \(q-1\) elements split into \(h=(q-1)/2\) negation classes.

So:

**Classification: HOLDS AS FAR AS I CHECKED.**

This is important because it makes Theorem 5.3 genuinely more general than the topological application.

# (b) How significant is this, honestly?

The strongest feature is clear: it would settle the Degtyarev–Shimada Conjecture 1.2 for **all odd prime-power degrees and all even dimensions**, whereas the original paper explicitly left the conjecture open.

That makes the result substantial, but its long-term impact will depend heavily on whether the partition theorem is seen as a reusable mechanism rather than a highly specialized device for this one Fermat problem.
