### T2 — B1, the count for even `m`. **HOLDS.**

*My derivation.* `a ∈ Γ` with witness `J`. Put `a_0 := (a_1⋯a_{n+1})^{−1}`. The pairs `i ≥ 1` cover `{1..n+1} ∖ {k_0}` and each has product `1`, so `a_1⋯a_{n+1} = a_{k_0}`, `a_0 = a_{k_0}^{−1} ≠ 1`, and the pair `{0, k_0}` is an inverse pair too. So `a ↦ (a_0, a)` maps `Γ` into the set `Σ` of `N`-tuples (`N = n + 2`) in `μ_m ∖ 1` that some perfect matching of `{0..n+1}` splits into inverse pairs. Conversely an element of `Σ` has product `1`, so its `0`-th entry is determined, and dropping it lands in `Γ` (same matching, written with `j_0 = 0`). Bijection. Now `S := μ_m ∖ 1` has `m − 1` elements; for even `m` inversion on `S` has exactly one fixed point (`−1`) and `(m − 2)/2` two-element orbits. A tuple splits into inverse pairs iff each orbit `{c, c^{−1}}` has equal multiplicities and `−1` has even multiplicity. Exponential generating function: `I_0(2x)^{(m−2)/2}·cosh x`. So `|Γ| = N!·[x^N] cosh(x)·I_0(2x)^{(m−2)/2} = N_{m−1}(N)`: the `N_r` of the mission with `r = m − 1` odd, `h = (m − 2)/2`.

### B2 — the chain at `m = q = 2^v`. **HOLDS** (all four steps, and the identification with Theorem O).

Setting: `F_2[G] = F_2[t_i]/(t_i^q − 1) = F_2[s_i]/(s_i^q) =: B`, `s_i = t_i + 1` (`t^q − 1 = (t + 1)^q`, `q = 2^v`). In `F_2[u]`, `(u + 1)φ(u) = u^q + 1 = (u + 1)^q`, so `φ(u) = (u + 1)^{q−1}`; with `u = t_jt_k`, `u + 1 = s_j + s_k + s_js_k`. So `ψ̄_J = Π_{i=0}^{k} s_{k_i}·Π_{i≥1}(s_{j_i} + s_{k_i} + s_{j_i}s_{k_i})^{q−1}`, and as a polynomial its component of lowest degree is `L_J := Π_{i=0}^{k} s_{k_i}·Π_{i≥1}(s_{j_i} + s_{k_i})^{q−1}`, homogeneous of degree `(k+1) + k(q−1)`.

1. `|Γ| ≥ dim I`: T1 at `p = 2`. (For `m = 2^v` the only prime is `2`, so the conjecture at `(k, 2^v)` is `dim I = |Γ|`.)
2. `dim I = dim in(I)`. `B` is graded (the ideal `(s_i^q)` is homogeneous). Let `I_{≥d}` be the elements of `I` with no component of degree `< d`; the linear map `I_{≥d} → B_d` (component of degree `d`) has kernel `I_{≥d+1}`, and its image is exactly `{0} ∪ {lowest components of the elements of I of order d}`, which is therefore a subspace, `in(I)_d`. Summing over `d` (finitely many): `dim in(I) = dim I`. `in(I) = ⊕_d in(I)_d` is an ideal: for a monomial `y` and `f` of order `d`, `y·f_d` is `0` or the lowest component of `yf ∈ I`.
3. `in(I) ⊇ (L_J)B`: the image of `ψ̄_J` in `B` is `L_J + (\text{components of higher degree})`; if `L_J ≠ 0` in `B` it is the lowest component of an element of `I`; with 2 (ideal) the inclusion follows.
4. `dim (L_J)B = dim (D_J)C_r`, `r = q − 1`. All `C(q−1, u)` are odd (`q − 1 = 2^v − 1` has all binary digits `1`), so `(a + b)^{q−1} = Σ_{u=0}^{q−1} a^u b^{q−1−u}` over `F_2`; times `b`, modulo `b^q`: `Σ_{u=1}^{q−1} a^u b^{q−u} = ab·Σ_{w=0}^{q−2} a^w b^{q−2−w} = ab·D_r(a, b)` over `F_2` (`D_r` has `r = q − 1` terms, signs invisible). The indices `k_0, j_1, k_1, …, j_k, k_k` are `1, …, 2k+1` once each, so in `B`: `L_J = Y·D_J`, `Y = s_1⋯s_{2k+1}`, `D_J = Π_{i≥1} D_r(s_{j_i}, s_{k_i})`. Multiplication by `Y` on `B` sends a monomial to a monomial or to `0`, and kills exactly those with an exponent `≥ q − 1 = r`; so it induces an injection `C_r = B/(s_i^r) → B`, `B`-linear, carrying `(D_J)C_r` onto `(Y D_J)B = (L_J)B`.

Conclusion: `|Γ| ≥ dim I = dim in(I) ≥ dim (L_J)B = dim_{F_2}(D_J : J)C_r`, and the right-hand ideal is literally the ideal of Theorem O at `(k, r = 2^v − 1, F = F_2)` (same ring `F[y_1..y_{2k+1}]/(y_i^r)`, same generators: the product over the `k` pairs of `J` avoiding `0`); `|Γ| = N_r(2k+2)` by T2. So Theorem O at `(k, 2^v − 1, F_2)` ⇒ Conjecture 1.2 at `(k, 2^v)`. I tried `v = 2`, `k = 1` by hand as the smallest cell: `q = 4`, `r = 3`, `D_3(a,b) = a² + ab + b²`, three generators in `F_2[y_1,y_2,y_3]/(y_i^3)`; nothing degenerates. For `v = 1` the chain is formally true and empty (`r = 1`, `C_1 = F_2`, `|Γ| = 1`) but [DS] need `m > 2`; the target excludes it (`v ≥ 2`).

The «circularity test» paragraph (that (O)≥ at `(k, 2^v−1, F_2)` is equivalent to «conjecture at `(k, 2^v)`» + «`in(I) = (L_J)B`») follows from the same chain and is right; it is not used.

### Lemma B.0 — the bound colouring by colouring. **HOLDS.**

*My derivation.* `m = q r'`, `q = p^v`, `p ∤ r'`. `G = G_q × G_{r'}` (both `(Z/·)^{n+1}`; `t_i = a_ib_i`, `a_i = t_i^x`, `b_i = t_i^y` with `x ≡ 1 (q)`, `x ≡ 0 (r')`, `y = 1 − x`). Let `K = Q_p(μ_{r'})` (unramified), `W` its integers, `F'` its residue field. `|G_{r'}|` is a unit of `W` and `W ⊇ μ_{r'}`, so the idempotents `e_c = |G_{r'}|^{−1}Σ_g c(g)^{−1}g` of the characters `c` of `G_{r'}` are in `W[G_{r'}]`, and `W[G] = ⊕_c W[G_q]e_c`, each summand free of rank `q^{n+1}`. For the ideal `I_W = (ψ_J)W[G]`: `I_W = ⊕_c e_cI_W`, and `e_cI_W` is a `W`-submodule of a free module over a discrete valuation ring, hence free of rank `ρ_c := dim_K(e_cI_W ⊗ K)`.
- *The rank.* Over `K̄`, `K̄[G]e_c` is the product of the lines of the characters `g` of `G` with `g|_{G_{r'}} = c`, i.e. of the `g ∈ μ_m^{n+1}` whose `μ_{r'}`-part is `c` (`g(b_i) = g_i^y` is the `μ_{r'}`-part of `g_i`). An ideal of a product of fields is the sum of the lines where some generator is non-zero: those with `ψ_J(g) ≠ 0` for some `J`, i.e. `g ∈ Γ` (the computation of Claim 4.3). So `ρ_c = #{g ∈ Γ : μ_{r'}\text{-part} = c} =: |Γ_c|`.
- *The reduction.* `W[G_q]e_c/p = F'[G_q]ē_c`; on it `b_i` acts as the scalar `c̄_i` and `a_i^q = 1`, so `(t_i − c̄_i)^q = c̄_i^q(a_i − 1)^q = 0`: it is the factor `R_c` of Lemma 6.1 of the paper, over `F'` (reduction is a bijection `μ_{r'}(W) → μ_{r'}(F')`). The image of `e_cI_W` there is the ideal generated by the images of `e_cψ_J`, i.e. `π_c(I)` (over `F'`; the dimension does not change over `F̄_p`).
- *The direction.* For a free `W`-submodule `M ⊆ L`, the image of `M` in `L/pL` is `M/(M ∩ pL)`, a quotient of `M/pM`, so its dimension is `≤ rank M`. Hence `dim π_c(I) ≤ |Γ_c|`. (Reduction can lose rank, never gain: the inequality goes the right way.)

It holds for `p = 2` with `r'` odd and for `p` odd with `r'` even alike; no parity is used. Summing over `c` gives back the global bound. I also checked the dependence on the product formula `|Γ_c| = N_1(c)·…`: that formula is not part of B.0 (it is checked under B3 «count factors» and B4).

Laplace expansion along the first `p` columns is the shuffle formula. `𝒜_n → Z[y]/(y_i^r)` is injective (exponents `≤ r − 1`).
- (5.2) *Divided powers.* For 2-forms, `φ^{∧k} = k!·γ_k(φ)` (terms commute, squares of terms vanish), `Λ^{ev}` is free, so `γ_k` is well defined and has the binomial rules. `φ^{∧k}` corresponds to the sum over *ordered* partitions into pairs, i.e. to `k!·Pf(f(y_i,y_j))`; divide by `k!`.
- (5.3) *Stable ideals.* `g ∧ γ_{k_1}(φ_1) ∧ γ_{k_2}(φ_2) ∧ ⋯` corresponds to `Σ ± g(y_X)·Pf_{f_1}(y_{Y_1})·Pf_{f_2}(y_{Y_2})⋯` over the ordered set partitions `(X, Y_1, Y_2, …)`, and each Pfaffian is a signed sum over matchings: an **integral** combination of images of the one polynomial `g·Πf_i` under permutations. No division by `k_i!` is needed, which is the point when pair forms coincide. (If equal forms are put in different groups one gets multiples, still in the ideal.)
- (5.4) *Pair forms.* `ω_s` is antisymmetric for odd `s` (coefficient of `a^ub^{u'}` is `(−1)^u`, of `a^{u'}b^u` is `(−1)^{u'} = −(−1)^u`). `ω_{r−2} = B`. `b^iD(a,b) = Σ_u(−1)^ua^ub^{r−1+i−u}`; the terms with `u ≤ i − 1` have `b`-exponent `≥ r` and die; the others (`i ≤ u ≤ r−1`) are all the terms of `ω_{r−1+i}`. `r − 1 + i` is odd iff `i` is odd, and `i = 1, 3, …, r − 2` gives `s = r, r+2, …, 2r − 3`: every odd `s ≥ r`. Parity basis: for `u < u'`, `u + u'` odd, `(−1)^uv_u ∧ v_{u'}` is (even index) `∧` (odd index) in both cases, so `Ω_{2σ+1} = Σ_{a+b=σ}e_a ∧ o_b` with `0 ≤ a ≤ h`, `0 ≤ b ≤ h−1`; `B ↔ J_{h−1}` (`r − 2 = 2(h−1) + 1`), high forms `σ ≥ h` ↔ `s ≥ r`.
- (5.5) `E(ζ) ∧ O(ζ) = Σ_{a,b}ζ^{a+b}e_a ∧ o_b = J(ζ)`; `E ∧ J = 0`, `J ∧ J = 0`, hence `γ_n(J(ζ)) = 0` for `n ≥ 2` (torsion-free).
- *Lemma 5.2.* From `0 = γ_n(L + ζ^hU) = Σ_jζ^{hj}γ_{n−j}(L)γ_j(U)` and induction; `γ_j(U) ∈ 𝔥_j[ζ]`, `𝔥_i𝔥_j ⊆ 𝔥_{i+j}` (`γ_kγ_{k'} = C(k+k',k)γ_{k+k'}`).
- *Corollary 5.3, powers of `ζ`.* (a) In `γ_{t+1}(Σ_{σ<h}ζ^σJ_σ)` the exponent `(t+1)(h−1)` is reached only by `γ_{t+1}(J_{h−1})`. On the other side, `ζ^{h(t+1)}A'_{t+1}` starts at `h(t+1) > (t+1)(h−1)`, and every coefficient of `ζ^{ht}L ∧ A_t` is in `Σ_{σ<h}J_σ ∧ 𝔥_t`. (b) `e_h ∧ γ_t(J_{h−1})` is the top coefficient (`ζ^{h+t(h−1)}`) of `E ∧ γ_t(L)`, and `E ∧ γ_t(L) = ζ^{ht}E ∧ (A'_t − U ∧ A_{t−1})` by Lemma 5.2 and `E ∧ L = −ζ^hE ∧ U`; all coefficients in `V ∧ 𝔥_t`.
- (5.6) *The proof.* `Pf_E(M)` is alternating in `M` with exponents `≤ r − 1`, and by (F7) it is `±v_{e_1} ∧ ⋯ ∧ v_{e_s} ∧ γ_κ(J_{h−1})` (`κ = t + 1` for `ℓ ≥ 1`, where `s = ℓ − 1`; `κ = t` for `ℓ = 0`, where the border is `v_{r−1} = e_h`). The count of degrees is right: `(ℓ−1) + 2(t+1) = ℓ + 1 + 2t`. `𝔘_ℓ(M) ⊇` the polynomials of `Λ^{ℓ+1}(V) ∧ 𝔥_t`: an alternating `g` in `ℓ+1` variables is `Δ(S)` times a polynomial (over `Z`), each high pair form is a multiple of `D` modulo the box, so `g(y_S)Πω(y_a,y_b) ∈ Δ(S)D_Q·Z[y]/(y^r)`, and (5.3) applies. Then Corollary 5.3 (a) (wedged with `v_0 ∧ ⋯ ∧ v_{ℓ−2}`: `(ℓ−1) + 2 = ℓ + 1`) and (b) (`1 = ℓ + 1`).
I checked the smallest case by hand, `r = 3`, `(ℓ, t) = (0, 1)`: `y_3^2(y_2 − y_1) − y_2^2(y_3 − y_1) + y_1^2(y_3 − y_2) = Δ(y_1,y_2,y_3)` and `y_1D(y_1,y_2) − y_1D(y_1,y_3) + y_2D(y_2,y_3) = Δ + y_2^3`. ✓.
