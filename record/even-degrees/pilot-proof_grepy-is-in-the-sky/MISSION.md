## 1. The object

Let `k ≥ 1`, `n = 2k`, `N = 2k + 2`, `m ≥ 3`, and let `X ⊂ P^{n+1}` be the Fermat variety `z_0^m + … + z_{n+1}^m = 0`.

- `𝒥` is the set of the `(2k+1)!!` perfect matchings of `{0, 1, …, 2k+1}`, written `J = [[j_0, k_0], …, [j_k, k_k]]` with `j_i < k_i` and `j_0 = 0`.
- For `J ∈ 𝒥` and `β = (β_0, …, β_k)` with `β_i^m = −1`, the standard `k`-space `L_{J,β} ⊂ X` is `z_{k_i} = β_i z_{j_i}`. `L(X) ⊆ H_n(X; Z)` is the subgroup generated by their classes.

> **Conjecture 1.2 of Degtyarev–Shimada [DS].** `H_n(X)/L(X)` is torsion free.

The algebraic form (from [DS]; see `material/THE_CHAISE_LONGUE_THEOREM_v10.md`, §2, which is written from the original):

- `R := Z[t_1, …, t_{n+1}]/(t_i^m − 1)`, the group ring of `G ≅ (Z/m)^{n+1}`; `φ(u) := 1 + u + … + u^{m−1}`.
- `ψ_J := Π_{i=0}^{k} (t_{k_i} − 1) · Π_{i=1}^{k} φ(t_{j_i} t_{k_i})`.
- `Γ ⊆ (μ_m ∖ {1})^{n+1}` is the set of the `a` such that, for some `J ∈ 𝒥`, `a_{j_i} a_{k_i} = 1` for `i = 1, …, k`.

> **Fact (paper, Theorem 0 and Proposition 2.1; valid for every `m ≥ 3`).** For every prime `p`, `dim_{F_p} (ψ̄_J : J ∈ 𝒥)·F_p[G] ≤ |Γ|`; and Conjecture 1.2 holds at `(k, m)` if and only if equality holds for every prime `p` dividing `m`.

So the conjecture at `(k, m)` is: **for every prime `p | m`, the ideal generated by the `ψ̄_J` in `F_p[G]` has dimension `|Γ|`.** Only the lower bound `≥` has to be proved.

## 2. What is proved, and what is open

- **PROVED (the paper in `material/`): every odd `m`, every `k`.** For `m = q = p^v` (§2–§5): in characteristic `p`, with `y := t − t^{−1}`, `F_p[G] = F_p[y]/(y_i^q)`, the relations become linear (`t_j t_k − 1 = (unit)·(y_j + y_k)`), and `ψ̄_J = (unit)·Y·D_J` with `Y = Π y_i` and `D_J` the product, over the `k` pairs of `J` that avoid `0`, of `D(a, b) = (b^{q−1} − a^{q−1})/(a + b)`. The conjecture becomes Theorem B: `dim (D_J : J)·F[y]/(y_i^{q−1}) = Q_k(q)`, **over every field `F` and for every odd `q`**. Its proof (§5, Theorem 5.3) is an induction that peels one variable and works with down-sets of partitions. For odd `m` that is not a prime power, §6 splits `F[G]` by «colours» (roots of unity of order prime to `p`) and §7 proves a bipartite count (Theorem C) by the same method with the box `q`.
- **OPEN: every even `m`, for `k ≥ 2`.** Problem 1 of §13 of the paper.
- **Known for even `m`:** `k = 1` for every `m` (Degtyarev; his paper is in `material/sources/`); by computer [DS, §5], `(n, m) = (4, m)` for `3 ≤ m ≤ 12` and `(6, 4)`.

**Why the proof for odd `m` does not run at the prime `2`.** Lemma 2.3 of the paper uses that `2` is a unit three times: `t + 1` is a unit; `y = t − t^{−1}` is a coordinate on which inversion is `y ↦ −y`; and `t_j t_k − 1 = (unit)·(y_j + y_k)`. In characteristic `2`, with `s := t − 1`: `t + 1 = s` is nilpotent, `y = (unit)·s^2` is not a coordinate, inversion is `s ↦ s/(1+s)` and is not a change of sign, and `t_j t_k − 1 = s_j + s_k + s_j s_k` is not linear. Also `−1 ∈ μ_m ∖ {1}` is fixed by inversion.

Rafa's image, which names the mission: the proof for odd degree is a car (it needs a steering wheel, grip and straight ground). For even degree he wants an aeroplane, which needs none of the three.

## 5. What died before (from the record of the project; do not fly there)

- **Duality.** Any statement obtained from (O) or from the conjecture through Gorenstein or Matlis duality of the box is the same statement in other words (the paper, Proposition 2.6, says so of its own intersection form).
- **Subfamilies of matchings.** The statements are false for subfamilies: for (O) at `k = 2`, `r = 3`, characteristic `3`, with 14 of the 15 matchings the dimension is `140` while the count of points is still `141`; and the paper (§9.2) gives a subfamily at `(k, q) = (2, 9)` for which the conjecture itself fails. So no argument that is local or hereditary on the family can work; the full family `𝒥` must be used (paper, §9).
- **Counting alone.** Hilbert functions do not decide these questions: two rings with the same Hilbert function can differ in the property wanted.
- **Steps in `q`.** Bounds of the kind `dim(3q) ≤ C·dim(q)` sharp enough to climb the tower are equivalent to the conjecture.
- **The ring of Theorem A in characteristic `2`.** See the warning in R4.
